# 2. Sentence Patterns

## 2.1 Negative parallelism

- **"Not only X, but also Y"** / "it is not just X, it is Y".
- **"Not X, but Y"** / "It's not X, it's Y" / "no X, no Y, just Z".
- **"Y rather than X"**: the reversed form, common in recent output. The guide lists *rather than* framings, which treat a claim as one thing rather than another, as a mid-2026 sign.

Each is acceptable once when the contrast carries real information. It becomes a sign when used repeatedly as a rhetorical device.

## 2.2 Rule of three

Triads of adjectives or short phrases ("fast, reliable, and scalable"; "discussions about X, Y, and Z") make a shallow point look complete. Triads that name three real, distinct items, such as three conditions or three classes, are content, not a sign.

## 2.3 Trailing -ing clauses (superficial analysis)

A main clause followed by an evaluative participle: "..., highlighting the importance of X", "..., underscoring ...", "..., ensuring ...", "..., reflecting ...", "..., contributing to ...", "..., fostering ...". These attach an opinion without evidence.

## 2.4 Avoiding the plain copula

"serves as", "stands as", "functions as", "represents", "marks" used where "is" would do; "boasts / features / offers" where "has" would do.

## 2.5 Uniform rhythm (low burstiness)

Detectors measure how much sentence length and predictability vary. AI text tends toward similar-length, similarly built sentences, and newer models toward many 8–20-word sentences. Human writing mixes short sentences with long ones.

## 2.6 Elegant variation

Repeatedly swapping synonyms for the same thing ("the model", "the system", "the framework", "the approach") because of repetition penalties. Technical writing should use one name for one thing.

## 2.7 Punctuation habits

- **Em dashes** where commas, colons or parentheses fit. This was a strong sign from 2022 to 2025, and newer models avoid it.
- **Overuse of colons and semicolons** to stitch two clauses together, giving a "punched-up" set-up/payoff rhythm.
- Curly quotes in plain-text contexts.

## 2.8 Set-up / payoff and signposting

- "The answer is simple: ..." / "Here's the thing: ..." / "The key insight is that ..."
- "It is important to note that ..." / "It is worth noting that ..."
- Restating each paragraph's point in its last sentence.

## 2.9 Sentence-initial connectors

Starting many sentences with Additionally / Moreover / Furthermore / Notably / Importantly / Overall / Ultimately. Each is harmless alone; a run of them is a pattern.
