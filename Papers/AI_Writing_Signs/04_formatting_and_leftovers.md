# 4. Formatting and Leftovers

## 4.1 Formatting habits

- Title Case for every heading in contexts that use sentence case.
- Heavy **boldface** for emphasis inside running text.
- Bulleted lists with a bold inline header ("**Speed:** ...") used everywhere, including where prose would read better.
- Emoji at the start of headings or list items.
- Wrong heading levels, such as several top-level headings.

**Note for IEEE papers:** IEEE conference style puts section headings in small capitals and table captions in title case. Both reference papers (*All Dice No Slice* and *Take the Hint*) use a bold-labelled contribution list in the introduction. These are venue conventions, not AI signs, and should match the reference papers.

## 4.2 Chatbot conversation left in the text

"Certainly!", "Of course!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if ...", "Would you like ...", "Here is a revised version ...", "As an AI language model ...", "as of my last knowledge update".

## 4.3 Placeholders and templates

Bracketed fill-ins ("[insert result]", "[Author Name]"), "XX" dates, `INSERT_SOURCE_URL`, `example.com/source1`, "TODO", "TBD", "Lorem ipsum".

## 4.4 Markdown residue

`**bold**` asterisks, `#` headings, `---` dividers, triple-backtick blocks, `[text](url)` links appearing in a document that does not use Markdown.

## 4.5 Tool artefacts

Exposed citation tokens such as `contentReference[oaicite:1]`, `utm_source=chatgpt.com` or `utm_source=openai` in URLs, ↩ back-link characters, and text cut off mid-sentence at a token limit.

## 4.6 Sudden change of style

A section written in a markedly different, more polished voice than the rest of the document, or than the author's earlier work.
