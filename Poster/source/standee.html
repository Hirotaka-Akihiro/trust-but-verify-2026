Trust, but Verify the Label

Encrypted-traffic misclassification as the residual risk of deterministic policy gating

Research question

An AI agent's actions pass a correct policy check. Which violations still get through, and why?

01 · Problem

Labels decide what is allowed

Encrypted flows are labelled from timing alone. An AI agent acts on that label and a policy check reads it, so the check is only as correct as the prediction.

02 · Gap

Accuracy hides costly errors

Classifier studies report overall accuracy, not which mistakes change an enforcement decision. Pre-execution guards assume their inputs are correct.

03 · The policy

Three levels of permission

P2P: block only
Streaming, file transfer: block, or limit ≤ 1,024 kbps
Browsing, chat, email, VoIP: allow, block, or limit ≤ 10,000 kbps

06 · Classifier

Better than the original baseline, still imperfect

On the dataset's original protocol: 0.855 weighted precision, 4.7 points above the rebuilt C4.5 (Fig. 3). Costliest error: 13.6% of file-transfer flows read as chat.

07 · Probability-aware gate

Catching label errors costs legitimate work

Reading class probabilities removes 5 of the 6 violations only at a 10% over-restriction budget (ρ = 0.25), and then just 43 of 50 permitted tasks complete (Fig. 4).

{{svg:fig5_tradeoff}}
Fig. 4. Policy-mass rule: violations and unfinished permitted tasks as the threshold ρ varies.
08 · Conclusion

A gate is only as correct as the label it reads

A deterministic gate stops most policy violations at microsecond cost; what gets through comes from classifier errors that move restricted traffic into a permissive class. Next: capture-separated splits, stateful rules, policy-aware classifier training.
